The big idea

Connected, not integrated

There are three ways to run the health information of a province. Pick one, then cause an outage and see what happens.

In shortTowns, hospitals and clinics keep what they built. They share common rules, a common language and a few national lists. That is how one province-wide team works without one giant computer system.

Where AI fits

Don't wait in line. Close the gap now.

The usual plan makes towns that start behind go step by step, and they stay behind. We use AI right where each gap is, while the foundations are built at the same time.

The usual way: one step at a time

  1. Put everything on paper in order
  2. Type it all into computers
  3. Connect the computers
  4. Hire analysts to study the data
  5. Only then, maybe, use AI

Each step waits for the last. Meanwhile, the goal keeps moving.

The leapfrog way: AI at every gap

  1. AI reads paper records into digital ones
  2. AI guides health workers where staff are few
  3. AI matches messy records and speaks local languages
  4. Leaders ask the scoreboard questions in plain words
  5. All while the shared road is being built

Like mobile phones that skipped landlines.

Honest limits

What AI can't do alone

AI needs the shared road

Without one health ID, shared lists and common rules, AI becomes a set of small, separate pilots. Build both at the same time.

People decide

AI extends health workers. It does not replace them. A person makes every decision about care and money.

Bad data, bad advice

That's why the first AI jobs are reading and cleaning records.

Privacy comes first

Everything follows the Data Privacy Act. Only people with a right to see a record can see it.

Fair to everyone

AI is checked for bias, so it works as well for a fisherman in Isla Verde as for an office worker in the city.

Shared, not bought alone

Shared provincial and national AI services keep it from becoming one more advantage for richer towns.

In shortWaiting in line keeps poor towns behind. AI placed at each gap, offered as a shared service, lets every town catch up and get ahead, with people still in charge.
